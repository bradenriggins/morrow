# Expert education article outlines

## 1. The unit of AI-assisted course work is a reviewed change

**Audience:** Instructional designers and course builders.

**Thesis:** A finished course update lets an educator see the exact course and proposed edit, decide before it changes, and check what the LMS saved. A generated draft alone cannot do that.

**Outline:**

1. Explain the difference between a useful draft and an edit in a live course.
2. Show the five practical facts a reviewer needs: the destination, current content, proposed content, likely consequence, and saved result.
3. Walk through a page correction and a related quiz implication without claiming automatic alignment.
4. Explain that approval applies to one clear proposed change, not later work.
5. Explain why the team checks the LMS again and needs a visible unconfirmed result when it cannot prove the change.
6. Show the privacy boundary: Canvas and Moodle sign-in can stay in Chrome while selected course content can reach a chosen assistant under its own data terms.
7. Give a one-page review checklist for any course edit that uses AI assistance.

**Morrow role:** Use one concrete example after the framework. Do not turn the article into a product announcement.

## 2. The source–lesson–assessment–learner path review

**Audience:** Faculty developers, instructional designers, and assessment leads.

**Thesis:** A content correction is not finished when the lesson text changes. The team also needs to check the related assessment and the learner path. An assistant can help find issues, but people keep the teaching judgment.

**Outline:**

1. Define the source, lesson, assessment, and learner path through the question each one answers.
2. Show how an inaccurate lesson claim can create a wrong assessment key.
3. Explain the difference between checking that content matches and deciding what students should learn.
4. Show why a correct page and quiz can still fail when a learner cannot reach them.
5. Provide a focused review worksheet for one Canvas module.
6. State the limits: rubrics, bank draws, accessibility, and learner access need separate evidence.

**Morrow role:** Describe the preview as one implementation of the framework, with current workflow limits beside it.

## 3. What a checked Canvas change needs

**Audience:** Learning-technology leaders and Canvas support teams.

**Thesis:** A course team needs to know that the right content reached the right place and is available to learners. An API response or green automation log does not establish that. Canvas must show what it saved.

**Outline:**

1. Distinguish a request being accepted, a provider responding, content being saved, and a learner being able to use it.
2. Explain why a team records the current course item before preparing a change.
3. Explain why approval must name the affected item and requested values.
4. Explain why a new LMS check should stop further action when it finds a mismatch.
5. Explain why an uncertain delivery needs inspection instead of another automatic write.
6. Provide a support-team evidence template for one Canvas change.

**Morrow role:** Use the product only to illustrate the concepts. Do not claim institutional-grade governance, universal compatibility, or multi-person approval.
